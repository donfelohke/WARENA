# Before Africa — Knowledge, Technology and Governance

**WARENA research note · 7 November 2026 · Status: RESEARCH**

## Purpose

Investigate the technologies, infrastructures and governance practices developed by African societies before and alongside external imperial and colonial classification. This is not a claim that one unified African technology stack or governance model existed.

## Research questions

- How did particular communities manage water, soils, seed, livestock, food storage, metallurgy, ceramics, textiles, construction and transport?
- What technical knowledge was embedded in specialist practice, apprenticeship, oral transmission and collective labour?
- How were trade routes, markets, ports, caravan networks and information flows maintained?
- How did political systems coordinate land, taxation, defence, justice, public works and dispute resolution?
- Which institutions were locally developed, borrowed, adapted or imposed?

## Technical domains to document

1. **Ecological infrastructure:** irrigation, terracing, water harvesting, pastoral mobility, fire management, soil and seed knowledge.
2. **Materials and production:** iron and copper working, gold, ceramics, textiles, architecture, shipbuilding and toolmaking.
3. **Information systems:** scripts, manuscripts, accounting, oral archives, genealogies, messenger networks, calendars and specialist knowledge.
4. **Trade and networks:** Indian Ocean, Red Sea, trans-Saharan, Nile, Sahelian, forest and regional exchange systems.
5. **Governance infrastructure:** councils, courts, lineage institutions, age grades, royal administrations, federations and decentralised systems.

## WARENA connection — an explicit analogy, not historical equivalence

WARENA's contemporary principles—local-first operation, reproducibility, inspectability, maintainability and collective governance—can be used as questions when studying historical systems. They must not be projected backwards as if historical communities used modern computing concepts. For each case, document actual mechanisms, materials, constraints, labour, ownership, access and failure modes.

A historical technology is not automatically sustainable, egalitarian or superior. Evaluate it in its context and distinguish demonstrated performance from later romantic claims.

## Research method

Use archaeological reports, archaeobotany, archaeometallurgy, historical linguistics, inscriptions, manuscripts, oral histories and environmental evidence. Record exact site, dating method, uncertainty, source, interpretation and counter-evidence. Triangulate rather than infer a whole society from one artefact.

## Evidence rule

Mark every statement VERIFIED, CORROBORATED, DISPUTED, UNVERIFIED or INTERPRETATION. A source file, proposed architecture or historical analogy is not proof of a working modern implementation. Keep WARENA's deployment claims separate from historical research.
